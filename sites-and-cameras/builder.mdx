Builder is where you configure the real-world spatial context of a site, cameras, calibration, points of interest, geofences, and facility floor plans, so Worlds can translate what your cameras see into real-world locations. It's meant to be built up iteratively: a site is usable as soon as cameras are placed and streaming, and you can layer in calibration, points of interest, geofences, and facilities over time as needed.

## Before You Start

- Sites & Cameras, and Builder within it, requires the Manager role or above. If your navigation only shows Monitoring, you won't have access.
- You don't need to complete every object type at once. Start with cameras, then add calibration, points of interest, geofences, and facilities as your use case calls for them.

## Step 1: Select or Create a Site

Under Sites, you'll see every site in your organization that you have permission to view, listed down the left side. Click the icon at the top to create a new site, or select an existing one. Selecting a site zooms the map to that location.

Once you're on a site, the same left panel shows every spatial object type associated with it:

- **Cameras** - the video sources connected to the space
- **Detectors** - the live models running inference across the site's cameras
- **Points of Interest** - geospatial points that group cameras together
- **Geofences** - polygons in lat/long space used to trigger workflow logic
- **Facilities** - floor plans for buildings at the site, usable today for camera calibration reference and mapped data visualization elsewhere in the product ([Overwatch](/monitoring/overwatch) support is coming soon)

**Tip:** Geofences are especially useful if you're integrating GPS devices into your Worlds data.

\[SCREENSHOT: Site selected, map zoomed with left panel object list\]

## Step 2: Edit Site Info or Launch Builder

Click the ellipses icon next to a site for two options: **Edit Site** and **Launch Builder**.

- **Edit Site** covers basic site information: name, location (by lat/long or by searching an address), and which storage account the site uses. Assigning storage accounts per site is useful if you want to separate storage costs by facility.
- **Launch Builder** is where the actual spatial configuration happens, and typically where you'll create and calibrate cameras.

\[SCREENSHOT: Ellipses menu - Edit Site / Launch Builder\]

## Step 3: Navigate the Builder Map

Inside Builder, the same object menu (Cameras, Detectors, Points of Interest, Geofences, Facilities) sits on the left to find, select, and edit existing objects. Use the **Insert** button at the top of the map to create a new camera, point of interest, geofence, or facility.

**Tip:** Scroll to zoom, left-click and drag to pan, and hold Ctrl while dragging to rotate the map. Getting the right vantage and zoom level makes placing and reviewing objects much easier.

\[SCREENSHOT: Builder map view with Insert menu open\]

## Step 4: Add and Position a Camera

Selecting or inserting a camera opens a right-side panel with its basic information: streaming URL, position, and rotation.

**Tip:** Get the position as accurate as possible, either by entering exact latitude and longitude or by clicking and dragging the camera on the map. Rotation only needs a rough estimate at this stage, point-to-point calibration (Step 5) will set the actual rotation value for you afterward.

Once a camera is placed, confirm it's streaming video before moving on to calibration.

\[SCREENSHOT: Camera panel showing streaming URL, position, and rotation fields\]

## Step 5: Calibrate the Camera

With a camera selected, find **Active Calibrations** at the bottom of the right panel and click the plus icon to start a new calibration. This pulls up a recent thumbnail from the camera's live stream in a panel below the map.

Use the **Add Marker** button to place matching points on both the map and the video thumbnail.

**Tip:** Reference static objects and artifacts, corners, doors, pillars, and place them as accurately as you can. This is what lets Worlds translate detections from that camera into real-world locations.

Once all markers are placed, click **Quick Save** to start the calibration job. When it completes, the camera is calibrated, and its detections will carry both a lat/long position and a pixel location within the camera frame.

\[SCREENSHOT: Calibration panel with markers placed on map and video thumbnail\]

## Step 6: Group Cameras with Points of Interest

Insert a point of interest from the top menu. It appears on the map as a point you can click and drag, similar to a camera. Use the right-side panel to select which cameras should be associated with it from a dropdown.

**Tip:** Points of interest aren't commonly needed, but they're valuable when several cameras share a single mount facing different directions, or when you want to group cameras across a larger outdoor site (different lots on a construction project, or different buildings on a larger campus) so you can quickly filter to just that group.

\[SCREENSHOT: Point of interest with associated cameras panel\]

## Step 7: Define Areas with Geofences

Insert a geofence from the top menu to create a polygon. Click a point to edit it, click a midway line to add a new point, or click and drag the rotate icon to reposition the whole shape.

Geofences define specific areas of physical space. They're especially useful alongside calibrated cameras with a wide field of view, common in outdoor settings, letting you build workflow logic that triggers when a detected object does or doesn't intersect the defined area.

\[SCREENSHOT: Geofence polygon on map\]

## Step 8: Upload Floor Plans with Facilities

Insert a facility from the top menu and give it a name, typically a building at the site. A single site can contain multiple facilities if it has multiple buildings.

Once created, a **Floors** menu appears on the right. Click to add a floor and you'll get an upload dialog for a floor plan image. After uploading, the image renders directly onto the map, where you can:

- Click the corners to rescale
- Click and drag to reposition or rotate
- Use the **Freeform** and **Locked** tabs to either warp the image to match points or scale it proportionally
- Use the opacity slider to see the mapping library's building outline underneath, to confirm alignment

**Tip:** Align the floor plan to the existing building outline in the mapping library as precisely as you can. Once aligned and saved, it becomes a reference for calibrating cameras against interior features (walls, structures, corners, doors), and adds spatial context to cameras, detections, and events elsewhere in the product.

Save the floor once you're satisfied with the alignment.

## Quick Reference

- **Select or create a site** - land on the right location and object list
- **Edit Site vs. Launch Builder** - basic info vs. actual spatial configuration
- **Add a camera** - accurate position, rough rotation
- **Calibrate** - match markers between map and video to translate detections to real-world location
- **Points of Interest** - group cameras, mainly for multi-camera mounts or large outdoor sites
- **Geofences** - define areas for workflow triggers, especially useful outdoors
- **Facilities** - upload and align floor plans for calibration reference and spatial context
